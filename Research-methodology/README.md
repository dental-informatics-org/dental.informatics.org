# Generalized Experiments  
## Field Experiments  
**Laboratory Experiments**  
> Conducted in controlled environments to isolate variables and test hypotheses under precise conditions.  

**Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs)**  
> Participants are randomly assigned to treatment or control groups to assess causal effects.  

**Cross-Sectional Surveys**  
> Collects data from a population at a single point in time to analyze variables or trends.  

**Longitudinal Surveys**  
> Repeated data collection from the same participants over an extended period to track changes.  

**Descriptive Surveys**  
> Focuses on summarizing characteristics or behaviors of a population.  

**Analytical Surveys**  
> Investigates relationships or associations between variables.  

**Causal Studies**  
> Examines cause-and-effect relationships, often through experimental manipulation.  

**Panel Studies**  
> Follows the same group of individuals over multiple time points to study trends.  

---

## Study Designs & Analysis  
### Time-Based Studies  
**Time-Series Analysis**  
> Analyzes data collected at successive intervals to identify patterns or trends.  

**Trend Analysis**  
> Focuses on detecting long-term directional changes in data.  

**Repeated Measures Design**  
> Collects multiple observations from the same subjects over time.  

### Correlation Studies  
**Positive Correlation**  
> Two variables increase or decrease together.  

**Negative Correlation**  
> One variable increases while the other decreases.  

**Zero Correlation**  
> No discernible relationship between variables.  

**Predictive Correlation**  
> One variable predicts changes in another, though not necessarily causal.  

### Causal Research Designs  
**Ex Post Facto Design**  
> Investigates causes retrospectively after effects have occurred.  

**Retrospective Causal Research**  
> Analyzes historical data to infer causal relationships.  

**Prospective Causal Research**  
> Tracks outcomes forward in time based on pre-existing conditions.  

**Regression Discontinuity Design**  
> Compares groups near a cutoff point to estimate causal effects.  

### Group Comparisons  
**Nonequivalent Control Group Design**  
> Compares non-randomized groups, often in quasi-experiments.  

**Group Comparisons**  
> Analyzes differences between predefined groups (e.g., demographics).  

**Matching Subjects Design**  
> Pairs participants based on key variables to reduce bias.

# Manipulates Variables to Test Causal Relationships  
## Experimental Settings  
**Real-world settings with manipulated variables**  
> Tests interventions in natural environments to enhance ecological validity.  

**Controlled conditions for precise manipulation**  
> Limits external influences to isolate variable effects.  

## Data Collection Methods  
**Cross-Sectional (One-time data collection)**  
> Snapshot data for immediate analysis.  

**Longitudinal (Follows participants over time)**  
> Tracks developmental or temporal changes.  

**Descriptive Surveys (Population characteristics)**  
> Provides a broad overview of a group’s attributes.  

**Time-Series Design (Regular interval observations)**  
> Monitors changes systematically over predefined periods.  

## Statistical & Analytical Techniques  
**Statistical Aggregation**  
> Combines data from multiple studies for meta-analysis.  

**Effect Size Evaluation**  
> Quantifies the magnitude of relationships or differences.  

**Systematic Review**  
> Rigorously synthesizes evidence from existing studies.  

**Publication Bias Analysis**  
> Assesses whether published results represent all available data.  

**Constant Comparative Method (Qualitative Coding)**  
> Iteratively compares data segments to develop themes or theories.

# Observational & Qualitative Research  
## Case Studies  
**Intrinsic Case Study**  
> Focuses on a unique case for its inherent significance.  

**Instrumental Case Study**  
> Uses a case to provide insight into a broader issue.  

**Collective Case Study**  
> Examines multiple cases to identify patterns or contrasts.  

**Single Case Study**  
> In-depth exploration of one subject, event, or organization.  

## Ethnographic Methods  
**Participant Observation**  
> Researcher immerses in a community to observe behaviors.  

**Cultural Immersion**  
> Engages deeply with a culture to understand norms and practices.  

**Field Notes Documentation**  
> Detailed records of observations, interactions, and reflections.  

**Reflexivity**  
> Acknowledges and mitigates the researcher’s influence on the study.  

## Phenomenological Approaches  
**Descriptive Phenomenology**  
> Captures lived experiences without interpretation.  

**Interpretive Phenomenology**  
> Explores meanings and interpretations of experiences.  

**Bracketing**  
> Researcher sets aside biases to focus on participants’ perspectives.  

## Qualitative Analysis Techniques  
**Thematic Analysis**  
> Identifies and interprets recurring themes in qualitative data.  

**Narrative Analysis**  
> Examines stories or accounts to understand structure and meaning.  

**Open/Axial/Selective Coding (Grounded Theory)**  
> Builds theories through iterative coding and categorization.  

**Chronological Analysis**  
> Organizes data by time sequence to identify trends.  

**Oral Histories**  
> Collects personal narratives to preserve historical or cultural insights.  

---

# Content & Document Analysis  
**Primary Source Analysis**  
> Examines original documents, artifacts, or records.  

**Secondary Source Synthesis**  
> Integrates findings from existing research or literature.  

**Thematic Content Analysis**  
> Identifies themes in textual or visual materials.  

**Quantitative Content Analysis**  
> Counts and categorizes specific elements (e.g., word frequencies).  

**Sentiment Analysis**  
> Determines emotional tone or attitudes in text (e.g., positive/negative).  

---

# Action Research & Reflective Practices  
**Participatory Action Research**  
> Collaborates with participants to solve practical problems.  

**Cyclical Process (Plan-Act-Observe-Reflect)**  
> Iterative cycles to refine interventions based on feedback.  

**Reflective Practice**  
> Continuous self-assessment to improve research processes.  

**Empowerment Focus**  
> Aims to create tangible, positive changes for participants.  

---

# Naturalistic & Structured Observation  
**Naturalistic Observation**  
> Observes behavior in uncontrolled, real-world settings.  

**Structured Observation**  
> Systematically records predefined behaviors or events.  

**Covert Observation**  
> Subjects are unaware they are being studied (reduces reactivity).  

**Overt Observation**  
> Subjects are informed about the observation (ethical transparency).
